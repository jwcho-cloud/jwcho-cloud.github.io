Nanophotonics & infrared optical materials.

I am a Scientist I at the University of Wisconsin–Madison in Professor Mikhail A. Kats' research group, focusing on nanophotonic structures and reconfigurable optical materials. My work spans material design, optical spectroscopy, ultrafast dynamics, electromagnetic modeling, and micro/nanofabrication to control light–matter interactions from the visible to far-infrared. Building on this, I develop tunable optical devices, energy-harvesting and thermal-management devices, and high-efficiency LEDs/OLEDs.

Beyond research, I enjoy exploring local restaurants, working out, and cooking.

Research Highlights

01

Infrared Photonics

I focus on designing photonic structures that interact with infrared light in a desired way. Specifically, I have been working on developing directional infrared sources that emit light with tailored directionality, passive radiative coolers that reject heat without any energy input, and tunable optical filters that use the phase transition of vanadium dioxide (VO₂) to selectively transmit or block infrared light on demand.

J.-W. Cho et al., ACS Nano 17, 10442 (2023) J.-W. Cho et al., Nano Lett. 22, 380 (2022) J.-W. Cho et al., Nano Lett. 21, 3974 (2021)

02

Material Engineering & Characterizations

Vanadium dioxide (VO₂) is a material that can switch its optical and electrical properties depending on its temperature. This topic explores how intentionally introducing defects into VO₂ through high-energy ion irradiation can precisely control this switching behavior (including optical contrast between the two phases, transition temperature, and hysteresis width) for tunable optical devices. I have been working on developing optical filters that switch at low energy thresholds, engineering the transition temperature across a wide range from −100 to 70 °C, and studying how defect engineering affects the intrinsic switching dynamics of VO₂.

J.-W. Cho et al., in preparation J.-W. Cho et al., in preparation

03

Multifunctional Photonic Surfaces

This topic focuses on designing optical surfaces that perform multiple functions at once. Using hollow micron-cavity structures as an optical platform, I have applied this architecture across a range of applications including light extraction in OLEDs, light absorption, and thermal emission control. By patterning surfaces into micron-scale grids, I have also built transparent electric heaters that achieve both uniform heat distribution and high optical transmittance, as well as coatings that are both antireflective and water-repellent.

S. Oh and J.-W. Cho et al., Adv. Sci. 9, 2202781 (2022) I. Yun et al., Nano Energy 93, 106857 (2022)

04

Optical Design of LED/OLED

A large fraction of light generated inside LEDs and OLEDs is trapped due to total internal reflection. I design nanoscale optical structures such as diffraction gratings with an air void inside to maximize diffraction efficiency, enabling more efficient light extraction compared to conventional filled grating patterns.

Y.-J. Moon and J.-H. Kim et al., Opt. Exp. 26, A450 (2018)
